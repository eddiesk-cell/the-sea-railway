# The places off the line

*Eddie's idea, 2026-07-27: "when we ride the train we do go past the important
scenes, but the rest we don't see — so when the user gets down and explores,
include more places according to the scenes."*

**Built, 2026-07-27.** Eighty-six of these are in the world: press **G** to get
down off the train and walk to them. What follows is the design they were built
to, kept as written because the three rules are the whole of it.

The train now stops. It holds where it is for as long as you are off it, and
**G** brings you back to it — a railway that leaves without you is a different
kind of story.

## Why this is the right next thing

A country on this line currently has **one picture**. That is correct for a
window seat — a train gives you one composition, held for twenty seconds, and
then it is gone. But it means each country is exhausted in twenty seconds, and
there is no reason to press **F** and walk.

Adding places changes what the world *is*. Right now it is a beautiful journey.
With this it becomes a place you go back to — and the feeling Eddie described,
*"oh, this is where —"*, is not something you can design directly. You can only
build the conditions for it: put the thing where it was, at the right scale, in
the right light, and say nothing. The recognition has to be the visitor's.

## The three rules

1. **The station keeps the headline image.** Whatever the film is on a poster,
   that stays out of the window. Nothing moves to make room.
2. **The rest is off the axis and out of sight.** Two hundred to eight hundred
   metres either side of the line, behind a ridge, round a headland, up a
   valley. You should have to *go*. A place you can see from the seat is not a
   discovery, it is scenery.
3. **Never label anything.** No signs, no captions, no "★ Deer God Pool". The
   whole pleasure is in recognising it unaided. The map may show that
   *something* is there — an unnamed mark — and no more.

Supporting mechanics this needs, in order of how much they matter:

- **A reason to stop.** Right now the train never stops. Stepping off at a
  station, and a whistle that brings it back, is phase 8's real work.
- **A trail of some kind.** Not a quest marker — a footpath, a stream to
  follow, a line of stone lanterns, a break in the trees. Ghibli always tells
  you where to walk without telling you.
- **Something that only happens when you arrive.** The pool lights up when you
  stand at its edge; the wind picks up at the top of the hill; the birds go up.
  One small change of state, so arriving is different from looking.

## What goes in each country

Ordered by stop. **Bold** is the thing already built and already out of the
window; the list under it is what should be found by walking.

### 1 · The Sea Railway — *Spirited Away*
**The bathhouse across the water.**
- The stone frog-shrine on the far bank, half sunk, where the road runs out
- The red bridge and the gate at the end of it, with lanterns coming on
- The pig sheds behind the town, lit and steaming
- Kamaji's boiler room — reachable, dark, a hundred wooden drawers
- Zeniba's cottage at the end of the line: one lamp, a garden, no bathhouse in
  sight. It should be a genuinely long walk.

### 2 · The Drowned Road — *Ponyo*
**The road under the tide and the shoal.**
- The house on the headland with the light in the window, and the path of
  stepping stones up to it
- The nursing home under the water, still lit, seen from above through the sea
- The tunnel at the bottom of the hill — the one nobody wants to go into
- The little boat with the candle-engine, tied where the road disappears

### 3 · The Marsh House — *When Marnie Was There*
**The house across the inlet at dusk.**
- The silo, inland, with the storm coming
- The rowing boat's landing on the far bank — you can get there the long way
  round, over the marsh, and stand where the water was
- The little blue house in the village where the letters are

### 4 · Poppy Hill — *From Up on Poppy Hill*
**The boarding house and the signal flags.**
- The Latin Quarter clubhouse: three storeys, dust, a skylight, stacks of paper
- The tug's berth down on the quay
- The long flight of steps up from the harbour, and the view back down it

### 5 · Koriko — *Kiki's Delivery Service*
**The town stacked to the clock tower.**
- The bakery with the flat above it and the window over the street
- The clock tower's own gallery, at the top, looking down the whole town
- Ursula's cabin in the woods behind the hill — a long walk, and worth it
- The pine on the cliff, and the sea below

### 6 · The Hidden Cove — *Porco Rosso*
**The inlet and the red seaplane.**
- The Hotel Adriano on its own island, terrace over the water, lit at night
- Piccolo's workshop up the canal: gantries, a half-built hull, tools
- The beach where the plane is hauled out on rollers
- The wrecked fuselage in the shallows, further round the headland

### 7 · Ocean Waves — *Umi ga Kikoeru*
**The sea wall and the crossing.**
- The school on the hill, empty, in the afternoon
- The airport bench
- The overhead footbridge where the two tracks cross, at dusk

### 8 · The Bus Stop — *My Neighbour Totoro*
**The lamp, the camphor, the rain.**
- The house with the veranda and the well, and the tunnel of camellia to it
- The forest tunnel down to the camphor's foot, and the hollow inside it
- The shrine with the stone foxes and the rope
- The flooded rice terraces from above, the whole valley of them
- The hospital, far off across the fields, with one window lit

### 9 · The Hillside — *Grave of the Fireflies*
**The pond and the fireflies.**
- The shelter by the water: a hollow, a blanket, a tin
- The burnt street, at the bottom of the hill, with nothing standing
- *Restraint is the tribute here. Two places, both quiet, nothing explained.*

### 10 · Safflower Fields — *Only Yesterday*
**The terraces and the safflower.**
- The farmhouse kitchen, from the yard, with the light on
- The mountain road at the top of the valley, and the view back over the whole
  farm at dawn
- The pineapple on the table — one small joke, for whoever gets it

### 11 · Tama Hills — *Pom Poko*
**The wood, and the line where it stops.**
- The temple in the last of the trees, with the racoon shrine
- The show-ground where the parade happened: an empty car park, at night
- The show-home estate, finished, occupied, and the woods behind it gone
- The one hillside they left

### 12 · The Rotary — *Whisper of the Heart*
**The railing, the shop, the town below.**
- The library, and the card in the back of the book
- The workshop under the shop: a violin on the bench, a clock with two figures
  that come out
- The overlook at the exact hour, when the town lights go off one street at a
  time

### 13 · The Cat Bureau — *The Cat Returns*
**The plaza under the flowerpot.**
- The Bureau itself: a front door you could cover with a hand, and it opens
- The crossroads where the cats queue
- The tower of the Cat King, further off, at full size — the joke reversed

### 14 · The Garden — *Arrietty*
**The grass, the clover, the watering can.**
- Under the floorboards: the borrowers' house, lit, with a pin for a rafter
- The doll's house room, perfect, in a room too big to see the end of
- The kettle in the stream, moored
- The brick wall, from below, with the ivy going up it like a cliff

### 15 · The Ink Country — *Kaguya*
**The mountains, the cloud, the bamboo.**
- The bamboo grove's centre, and the one stalk that is lit from inside
- The mansion in the capital: too large, too formal, and no one in it
- The hill above the village, where the running happens — and it should be
  possible to run down it

### 16 · The Cedar Forest — *Princess Mononoke*
**The columns, the river, the lights in the trunks.**
- **The Deer God's pool** — the water Eddie named. A round lake, an island in
  it, water so clear the bottom is a mirror, and everything round it silent.
  This is the best single place on the whole line and it should be the furthest
  to walk to.
- The cave under the roots
- The place the kodama gather, and they should follow you back

### 17 · Iron Town — *Princess Mononoke*
**The walled works on the lake.**
- Inside the gates: the bellows floor, the women working the treadles, the
  furnace mouth
- The stripped hill from the top, looking back down at the town
- **Ashitaka's village** — Eddie named this too. It belongs at the far end of
  the region: a palisade, a watchtower, terraces, and it should feel a long
  way from everything, because it is.

### 18 · The Meadow — *Howl's Moving Castle*
**The flowers, and the castle walking.**
- The castle itself, stopped, with the door you can go in
- The flower field with the lake and the cottage
- The waste, at the other end: bare, grey, and the castle's tracks across it

### 19 · Market Chipping — *Howl's Moving Castle*
**The hill town, the bunting, the tram.**
- The hat shop, and the workroom behind it
- The alley where the street is steepest
- The palace, far off across the valley, absurdly large

### 20 · Hort Town — *Tales from Earthsea*
**The walls, and the tower on the island.**
- The market street inside the walls, at noon, with the awnings
- The farm outside the town: a well, a wall, a field being worked
- The tower's island, reachable, and the door at the foot of it

### 21 · The Crooked House — *Earwig and the Witch*
**The lit door in the rain.**
- The workroom: shelves, jars, and a great deal of labelled nonsense
- The band's van, parked in a lay-by further up the lane

### 22 · The Valley of the Wind — *Nausicaä*
**The windmills and the forest edge.**
- The greenhouse under the castle: clean, warm, and growing the forest on
  purpose
- Inside the forest: the floor of it, under the caps, where the air is clear
- The acid lake, and the shell of something enormous half in it

### 23 · Slag Ravine — *Castle in the Sky*
**The terraces and the viaduct.**
- Down the shaft: the crystal cave, and it should light up when you get there
- The railway yard at the top of the incline
- The ruined fort on the ridge above the town

### 24 · Laputa
**The island, the root, the vines.**
- The garden level: grass, a pond, birds, and the robot standing in it with
  moss on him
- The tomb at the centre, and the tree's root going through it
- The underside, from below: the black stone, and the storm

### 25 · The Meadow of 1920 — *The Wind Rises*
**The grass, the easel, the glider.**
- The hangar at the field's edge, doors open
- The hotel in the mountains, veranda, in summer
- The dream field — the one where the aircraft are wrong and beautiful and go
  on to the horizon

### 26 · The Tower — *The Boy and the Heron*
**The tower in the wood.**
- Inside the door at the foot of it, which should go somewhere else entirely
- The sea gate, and the boat drawn up on the shingle
- The house with the corridors and the closed rooms

### 27 · The Sketch — *My Neighbors the Yamadas*
**White paper and four drawn things.**
- Nothing. This is the last stop and it is a blank page on purpose. The one
  addition: walk far enough off the line and the drawing runs out — no ground,
  no horizon, just paper — and that is where the line loops back to the first.

## How much work this is, honestly

About 90 places. At the density of the current regions that is roughly the same
again as everything built so far, and it should not be done in one pass. The
order I would take it:

1. ~~**Stepping off**~~ — done. **G** gets you down, holds the train, and puts
   you on the ground at walking pace with your feet on the actual floor.
2. ~~**Three places, in one country**~~ — done, and Mononoke was the right test.
3. ~~**Then the rest**~~ — done: eighty-six places across all twenty-seven
   countries, each with a path leading to it and a name that only arrives once
   you are standing in it.

What is NOT done, and is the honest state of it: the places are first passes.
They are built, reachable, named on arrival and lit correctly, but only a
handful have been looked at from the ground one at a time. Expect to find
things standing slightly wrong.
